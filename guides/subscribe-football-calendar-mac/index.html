Subscribe to a Football Calendar on Your Mac

Calendar guides · By CoreForge LLC · Published November 5, 2026

The Calendar app on a Mac has the most complete controls for subscribed calendars, including a setting for how often it checks for changes. This guide shows how to add a feed from the College Football Calendars or NFL Calendars and how to make the subscription reach your other Apple devices.

Before you start: copy the calendar link

Open the calendar site in your browser and choose the calendar you want. For All FBS, Top 25 and All NFL, each card has a Subscribe link and a Download .ics button. For a conference, division or team, use the picker and press Get link.

You want the web address of the feed, not a downloaded file. The easiest way to get it is to Control click (or right click) the Subscribe link and choose Copy Link. The address will begin with webcal:// or https:// and end in .ics. Either form works in the Calendar app.

If you click the Subscribe link directly in Safari, your Mac will usually offer to open Calendar and add the subscription for you. That is the shortcut. The manual route below gives you more control over the settings, which is worth the extra minute.

Add the subscription in Calendar

  1. Open the Calendar app.
  2. In the menu bar, choose File, then New Calendar Subscription.
  3. Paste the feed address into the box and click Subscribe.
  4. A settings window appears. Fill it in using the guidance in the next section, then click OK.

If the window says the subscription could not be found, check that you pasted the whole address with no spaces at the start or end, and that you copied the link to the feed itself rather than the page that lists the calendars.

Choose your settings

Name and color

Give the calendar a clear name, such as "Georgia football" or "49ers schedule". The color is how you will pick it out among other calendars, so choose one you are not already using for work or family.

Location

This choice matters more than it looks. If you pick iCloud, the subscription is stored with your Apple Account and normally appears on your iPhone, iPad and other Macs signed in to the same account, with no extra setup. If you pick On My Mac, the subscription stays on that one computer. For a football calendar that you want everywhere, iCloud is usually the better pick.

Auto refresh

The pop up menu lets you choose how often your Mac checks the feed for changes, from every few minutes to every week, or not at all. The football feeds are rebuilt nightly, so there is no benefit to checking every five minutes. Every day matches the pace of the data and keeps your Mac quiet. During a busy stretch, such as the weekend a flexed kickoff time is announced, you can choose a shorter interval and set it back later.

Remove options

You may see options to remove alerts or attachments that come from the feed. The football feeds are simple, with a title, a description and a start and end time, so there is little to strip out. Leave these at their defaults.

Check that it worked

Look at the sidebar of the Calendar app. If you do not see a sidebar, choose View, then Show Calendar List. The new calendar should appear under the location you picked, with a check mark beside it. Switch to the month view and move to a Saturday or a Sunday during the season. You should see games listed as events, each with a title showing the teams. A finished game also shows the final score.

Event times are stored in a universal time standard, and Calendar converts them to the time zone your Mac is set to. That means a game that kicks off at noon on the East Coast will appear at 9 a.m. for a Mac set to Pacific time. If a time looks wrong, check the time zone in your system settings before assuming the feed is off. The troubleshooting guide goes deeper on time zone surprises.

Changing your mind later

Control click the calendar in the sidebar and choose Get Info to rename it, change its color, or change the auto refresh interval. You can also uncheck it to hide the games without losing the subscription, which is a good habit in the off season. To remove it, Control click the calendar and choose Unsubscribe or Delete. Doing so never touches events you created yourself.

Subscribing to more than one feed

Many fans follow one favorite team plus a bigger picture. A common pairing is a single team calendar for the games you will actually plan around, plus the Top 25 or a conference calendar for context. Subscribe to each one separately and give them different colors, so you can switch the wider calendar off on busy weekends. Two overlapping feeds can show the same game twice, because each subscription is its own calendar. That is normal, and the fix is to hide or delete one of the overlapping subscriptions.

Why the Download .ics button is not the same thing

The download button saves a file that you can double click to import. Importing copies the events into a calendar of your choice at that moment. After that, nothing connects those events to the website. If a kickoff time is changed by a network, your imported copy will not change. A subscription, on the other hand, is a standing link that your Mac keeps checking. For a season long schedule, a subscription is almost always what you want. The file is useful only for a quick one off snapshot.

Quick checklist

  • Copy the feed address, or click Subscribe in Safari for the shortcut.
  • Use File, then New Calendar Subscription.
  • Choose iCloud if you want the calendar on your other Apple devices.
  • Set Auto refresh to every day.
  • Rename and recolor the calendar.
