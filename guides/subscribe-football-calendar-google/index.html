Add a College Football or NFL Calendar to Google Calendar

Calendar guides · By CoreForge LLC · Published November 5, 2026

Google Calendar can subscribe to the same feeds that power the College Football Calendars and the NFL Calendars. There is one quirk that trips people up: Google only lets you add a calendar by link from a computer, not from the phone app. This guide covers the whole path, including how to get the calendar onto your phone afterward.

Step 1: copy the feed link

On the calendar site, choose the calendar you want. For All FBS, Top 25 and All NFL, copy the link behind the Subscribe button. For a conference, division or team, pick it from the list and press Get link.

Google Calendar expects a normal web address. The Subscribe buttons use an address that starts with webcal://, which is meant for calendar apps. If Google rejects it, replace webcal:// with https:// at the start and leave the rest exactly the same. The Download .ics button points at the https:// version of the same feed, so you can also copy the link from that button instead of downloading the file.

Step 2: add it on the Google Calendar website

  1. On a computer, go to Google Calendar in your browser and sign in.
  2. In the left column, find Other calendars and click the plus icon next to it.
  3. Choose From URL.
  4. Paste the feed address into the box. If you are shown an option about making the calendar publicly accessible, leave it unchecked. It is not needed, because the feeds are public already.
  5. Click Add calendar.

The calendar appears in the left column under Other calendars, usually named after the feed. Click the three dots beside it to rename it, change its color or adjust notification settings. If the calendar looks empty at first, give it a little while. Google fetches the feed in the background, so the games sometimes do not show up for several minutes after you add it.

Step 3: get it on your phone

Android

The calendar belongs to your Google account, so the Google Calendar app on an Android phone signed in to the same account should show it automatically. If it does not, open the app menu, go to Settings, find the football calendar in the list and make sure it is switched on to show and sync. Subscribed calendars sometimes start switched off on a phone.

iPhone and iPad

If you use the Google Calendar app on an iPhone, the same rule applies: sign in with the same account and check that the calendar is turned on in the app's settings. If you prefer Apple's built in Calendar app, you can either add the Google account in the iPhone's settings and turn on calendars, or skip Google altogether and use the iPhone guide to subscribe directly. Going direct is often simpler because Apple Calendar lets you control the refresh behavior, while Google decides on its own schedule.

Be realistic about update speed

This is the part that surprises most people. Google Calendar refreshes subscribed calendars on its own timetable, and it does not give you a button to force a refresh. In practice, changes can take many hours to appear, and sometimes closer to a day. The football feeds are rebuilt every night and ask apps to check back about once a day, so day to day this is fine. It does mean that if a kickoff time changes in the afternoon, your Google Calendar might not reflect it until later. For the games you are really planning around, double check the time on the source site on game week.

Removing and re adding the calendar does not usually speed things up, and it can briefly show an empty calendar, so resist the urge unless you have a real problem. If the calendar seems stuck, the troubleshooting guide lists what to try in a sensible order.

Reminders and notifications

Google lets you set default notifications for each calendar in its settings. The football feeds do not include reminders, so you can add one yourself, for example a notification 30 minutes before each game or a notification the morning of. Open the calendar's settings from the left column, find the notification section and add what you want. This is a good way to avoid noisy alerts for a league wide calendar while still getting a nudge for your own team. Because the whole point of a league wide calendar is breadth, you may prefer to switch notifications off there and turn them on only for a team calendar.

Keeping the clutter down

A league wide calendar can add hundreds of events. A few habits help:

  • Use a team calendar for the games you plan around, and keep the broader calendar for glancing at the week ahead.
  • Give each football calendar a color that is distinct from your work and family colors.
  • Untick the calendar in the left column when the season ends. The subscription stays, and the events disappear from your views.
  • If you share your own calendar with other people, note that subscribed calendars you added are yours alone. Sharing your calendar does not share the football games.

Time zones

The feeds store every game at a fixed universal time, and Google shows it in the time zone set in your Google Calendar settings. If a game seems to start at the wrong hour, check the time zone under Settings, then General, then Time zone, and also check the time zone set on your phone. A traveler who flies from New York to Los Angeles will see games shift by three hours, which is correct behavior, not an error.

Removing the calendar

On the Google Calendar website, point at the calendar in the left column, click the three dots and choose Settings. Scroll to the bottom and choose Unsubscribe. This removes only the football calendar from your account. Nothing you created yourself is affected.

Quick checklist

  • Add by URL from a computer, since the phone app cannot do it.
  • Swap webcal:// for https:// if Google rejects the link.
  • Make sure the calendar is switched on in your phone app.
  • Expect updates within about a day, not within minutes.
  • Add your own notifications, because the feed has none.
