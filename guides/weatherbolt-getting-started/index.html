Getting Started with WeatherBolt: Radar, Forecasts and Pro

WeatherBolt guides · By CoreForge LLC · Published November 5, 2026

WeatherBolt (formerly HarborCast) is a weather app for iPhone built around three jobs: showing you rain and storms coming, telling you what each hour will be like, and, if you want it, covering tides and marine conditions for time on the water. This guide walks through the main pieces in the order most people use them, and explains what is included for free and what needs Pro.

Start with the radar

Open the radar first, because it answers the question most people actually have: is rain coming, and when? WeatherBolt plays back the last 2 hours of rain radar, so you can watch which way a band of rain has been moving and how fast. A layer for clouds can be added as well. Both are part of the free version.

Here is a simple way to use the playback. Watch the 2 hours of motion, then find your location on the map. Look at the direction the rain is traveling and estimate how long it will take to reach you, based on how far it moved during the replay. A band that crossed a quarter of the screen in 2 hours is not going to arrive in 10 minutes. Radar shows where rain is now and where it has been. It cannot promise that a growing storm will follow the same path, so pair it with the forecast rather than relying on the radar alone.

Read the hourly forecast

The forecast is organized by the hour, with today and the next 3 days included in the free version. Pro shows all 7 days. The hourly view is the more useful one for planning a specific outing, since a day that is "mostly sunny" can still hide a stormy hour at 4 p.m.

When you look at the hourly rows, check the following in order:

  1. The hour you will be outside. Not the daily summary.
  2. Rain chance and timing. Rain that arrives late in your window is easier to work around than rain at the start.
  3. Wind. Wind matters for cycling, running, boating and even for a patio dinner.
  4. The rating. More on this below.

Understand the Good, Caution and Danger ratings

WeatherBolt rates each hour as Good, Caution or Danger, based on limits that you set. This is a personal system rather than a universal one. A wind speed that a sailor finds delightful could ruin a paddleboard trip, so the app lets you choose the numbers that make sense for your activity. Saving your own limits is a Pro feature. The idea is simple: you decide in advance where the line is, and the app then marks the hours on the right side of it. That is much easier than doing mental arithmetic every time you check the forecast. Our guide to planning a day on the water shows how to choose the numbers.

Save the places you care about

The free version includes one saved place, and Pro supports up to 10. Most people use the first slot for home. If you add more, consider these:

  • The place where you keep a boat or launch a kayak
  • A cabin, a campsite or a family member's town
  • The field or course where you play

Saved places make it quick to compare conditions. A coastline can have a calm morning at one harbor and a rough afternoon a few miles away, so checking each place separately is worth the extra tap.

Lightning and storm tools (Pro)

Pro unlocks live lightning strikes, shown on the map with their distance and age marked by color. It also adds lightning alerts that work when the app is closed, and a storm tracker for hurricanes and tropical storms. The free version has a short preview of lightning, so you can try how it looks before deciding. The lightning detection comes from a NOAA satellite lightning detector, and its location is approximate, so treat it as awareness and not as a precise warning system. The full details are in the guide on lightning alerts and storm tracking.

Tides and marine (optional)

If you spend time on the water, you can turn on Tides & marine in Settings. It adds a tide chart, times for high and low tide, the marine forecast, and small craft advisories. It stays switched off unless you choose to turn it on, so the app is not cluttered if you only want everyday weather. If you are new to marine forecasts, How to Read a Marine Forecast explains the key numbers, including why gusts and wave period matter more than they first appear.

On your wrist and on your Home Screen

There is an Apple Watch app with complications for wind, tide and a day rating clock, and there are widgets for the Home Screen. These are useful when you want an answer in a glance, such as checking the rating for the next few hours before heading out the door.

Where the data comes from

Forecasts, tides and marine information come from NOAA and the National Weather Service, and satellite imagery is from NASA. Lightning comes from a NOAA satellite lightning detector. WeatherBolt is not endorsed by NOAA. The app also uses data from buoy stations for marine conditions. Because the data comes from public sources, it can be delayed or unavailable from time to time, and the app cannot fix problems at the source.

What is free and what is Pro

Free

  • Rain radar with 2 hours of playback
  • A clouds layer
  • The hourly forecast, plus today and the next 3 days
  • One saved place
  • A short preview of lightning

Pro

  • Live lightning strikes
  • Lightning alerts that work when the app is closed
  • The storm tracker and storm alerts
  • All 7 days of forecast
  • Saving your own limits
  • Up to 10 saved places

The app has no ads, and you do not need an account. Pro is an in-app purchase handled by Apple, and you can restore it from Settings if you move to a new device.

Privacy in one paragraph

WeatherBolt stores your places and preferences on your device. Weather requests are made directly from your phone to public data sources. The one exception is the optional lightning alert feature: if you turn it on, your device's push token and an approximate, city level location are sent to the alert server so it can notify you of nearby lightning. You can read the details in the WeatherBolt privacy policy.

A safety reminder

WeatherBolt is an informational tool. For navigation and safety decisions on the water, check official NOAA and Coast Guard advisories, carry the right safety equipment and use your own judgment. A good rating is not a guarantee, and conditions can change faster than any app can keep up.

Quick start checklist

  • Watch the 2 hour radar playback before every outing.
  • Check the hourly forecast for the hours you will actually be outside.
  • Save your most important place first.
  • If you boat or paddle, turn on Tides & marine.
  • Set your limits, if you have Pro, so ratings match your own comfort.
