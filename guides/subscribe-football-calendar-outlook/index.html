Add a College Football or NFL Calendar to Outlook

Calendar guides · By CoreForge LLC · Published November 5, 2026

Outlook can follow the same live feeds that the College Football Calendars and NFL Calendars publish, so game days sit alongside your meetings. Outlook comes in several versions, and the menu names differ between them, so this guide covers the three you are most likely to be using.

Step 1: copy the feed link

On the calendar site, pick the calendar you want. For All FBS, Top 25 and All NFL, copy the link from the Subscribe button. For a conference, division or team, choose it from the list, press Get link and copy the result.

Outlook is happiest with an address that begins with https://. The Subscribe buttons use webcal://. If Outlook complains about the link, change the first part from webcal:// to https:// and keep the rest identical. The Download .ics button links to the https:// form, so you can also copy the link from that button instead of saving the file.

Option A: Outlook on the web

This is the Outlook you reach through a browser with a Microsoft account or a work or school account.

  1. Open the calendar view in Outlook on the web.
  2. Choose Add calendar in the left column.
  3. Choose Subscribe from web.
  4. Paste the feed address into the Link to the calendar box.
  5. Give the calendar a name and pick a color. You may also be asked to pick a charm, which is only a small icon.
  6. Choose Import.

The calendar should show up in your list of calendars within a moment. Because it belongs to your account, it also appears in Outlook apps on your phone once they sync. Some work or school accounts have administrators who switch off internet calendar subscriptions. If the option is missing or the import fails right away, that policy may be the reason, and you may be better served by a personal account for this calendar.

Option B: classic Outlook on Windows

The long standing desktop version has a slightly different path.

  1. Switch to the Calendar view.
  2. On the Home tab, choose Add Calendar, then From Internet.
  3. Paste the feed address and select OK.
  4. Outlook asks whether you want to add the calendar and subscribe to updates. Choose Yes.

The calendar then appears in your list under Other Calendars. If you choose No or pick an import option instead, you may end up with a one time copy of the games that never updates. If that happens, remove it and subscribe again. The classic app lets you adjust how often it checks for updates by right clicking the calendar and opening its properties, where you may find an update limit setting for internet calendars. Daily is plenty for these feeds.

Option C: the newer Outlook for Windows and Mac

The newer Outlook apps share much of their design with Outlook on the web. Open the calendar view, choose Add calendar, and look for Subscribe from web. The steps from Option A apply: paste the address, name it, and import it. If you do not see that option, check which account is selected in the sidebar, because some account types, such as certain work accounts, do not offer web subscriptions.

What to expect afterward

Subscribed calendars in Outlook are read only. You can hide them, rename them, change their color and set up your own view, but you cannot drag games to new times. Each game appears with a title showing the teams. After the game ends, the title and description carry the final score. NFL games also list the TV network in the description.

Outlook refreshes internet calendars on a schedule it decides, and that schedule can be slow. The football feeds are rebuilt every night and ask calendar apps to check back about once a day, so a delay of several hours is normal and not a sign that something is broken. If a game has moved and Outlook still shows the old time, check the source site for the current schedule and give Outlook a day.

Mixing work and football without confusion

A football calendar sharing space with business meetings can cause awkward moments, so a few small decisions help.

  • Keep it personal. The subscription belongs to you, and your colleagues do not get a football calendar of their own just because you subscribed. If you share your work calendar, check your sharing settings so you know what others can view.
  • Use a distinct color. A bright color for game days makes them stand out from the sea of meeting blocks.
  • Hide it when you share your screen. Untick the calendar in your calendar list before a presentation, and the games will disappear from view until you tick it again.
  • Prefer a team calendar to a league calendar if your day is already crowded. Hundreds of events from a league wide feed can bury what matters.

Time zones

The feed stores every game at a fixed universal time, and Outlook converts it to the time zone in your settings. A noon kickoff in the East shows as 9 a.m. in the Pacific time zone. If your Outlook shows a surprising time, check the time zone in Outlook's calendar settings and in your operating system. If you travel often, remember that your calendar view will shift with the setting, which is correct.

Removing the calendar

In Outlook on the web, find the football calendar in the left column, open its menu with the three dots, and choose Remove. In the classic Windows app, right click the calendar under Other Calendars and choose Delete Calendar. Removing it only affects the subscription. Your own meetings and events remain untouched.

Quick checklist

  • Copy the feed link and use the https:// form if Outlook rejects the first one.
  • On the web, choose Add calendar, then Subscribe from web.
  • In the classic Windows app, choose Add Calendar, then From Internet, and answer Yes.
  • Expect updates about once a day.
  • Pick a team calendar if your schedule is already full.
